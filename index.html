FILLING IN THE BLIND SPOT — triangle rain edition

A copycat of the textbook CD demo, re-drawn in Coattails colours: a field of small flickering triangles, and a clean round hole in it. Position the hole on your eye's blind spot, hold your gaze on the fixation dot, and your visual system will draw the triangles straight across the hole — completing a pattern it cannot actually see.

330 px 58 px 65%
  1. Sit about 50–60 cm (roughly arm's length) from the screen, head still, screen centred in front of you.
  2. Cover one eye (right eye open = default) and press Find my blind spot: a small white disc waits down-field from the fixation dot. Slide Hole distance slowly until the disc vanishes completely while you stare at the dot. That's your blind spot.
  3. Press Start the rain. Keep staring at the dot — do not glance at the hole. Within 5–20 seconds the empty circle floods with triangles and disappears into the field.
  4. If the hole won't vanish, move a little closer or farther, or nudge the distance slider. Everyone's spot sits a little differently; the demo textbook used the same hunt.

Why it works: where the optic nerve leaves the eye there are no light receptors, so that patch of the world sends no signal at all. Faced with a simple, consistent pattern around the gap, the brain fills it in — drawing the surrounding texture across the hole rather than admitting a blank. A lattice is the most fillable pattern there is. Triangles respectfully stand in for the original's dots; the physiology is unchanged.

Adapted from the VisualLab companion software for Sensation and Perception (8th ed.) by E. Bruce Goldstein.

SECOND DEMO — THE LINE THAT DOESN'T BREAK

The companion classic, and the one a lattice artist feels in his bones. Below, a set of long horizontal lines runs the full width of the field — but the bold middle line has a gap cut out of it. Position that gap on your blind spot, exactly as you did for the hole above, and the gap closes: the line reads as one unbroken stroke, completed by the same filling-in, because the brain would rather continue a line than report a break in it.

330 px 116 px
  1. Same posture as above: arm's length, one eye covered, head still.
  2. Stare at this demo's fixation dot and slide Gap distance until the white locator (press Find my blind spot) vanishes — the gap now sits on your blind spot.
  3. Hide the locator and look at the bold line: the missing piece is simply not missing. Glance directly at the gap and it reappears instantly — proof the completion was yours, not the screen's.

Surveyor's note: this is the drawn-vertex problem wearing a lab coat. A line that should break, doesn't — because the perceiver completes it. Exactness in the work matters precisely because the eye is this willing to help.
